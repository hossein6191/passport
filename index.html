Passport

an on-chain record of behaviour

An operator registers an agent and a few claims. Five validators each talk to the agent themselves, then agree — or refuse — with three words per claim.

This is not a proof of which model sits behind an endpoint; no such proof exists. It is five independent observers agreeing on behaviour, and it says inconclusive whenever the behaviour did not settle the question. Every passport, issued or refused, is on the explorer with the validators' reasons.

01 How a passport is issued

step one · operator

Register the agent

An HTTPS endpoint and at most six claims from a closed set. A claim the battery cannot test is refused at registration.

step two · consensus

Every validator runs the battery

Each one POSTs the same public probes to the endpoint itself. Nothing the leader saw is trusted. Judged probes are asked in two presentation orders.

step three · three words

One word per claim

matches · contradicts · inconclusive. The validator compares its word with the leader's word, per claim, exactly.

outcome

Issued, refused or pending

Every claim matches → issued, valid 30 days on the transaction's own clock. Any contradiction → refused, with the reasons stored. Otherwise pending; ask again.

anyone, free

One question, no model

is_valid(agent, claim) — a synchronous view with no consensus, so an escrow can pay a passport-holder and nobody else.

04 Inspect or challenge — the calls that cost consensus

Inspect belongs to the operator: any outcome applies, and a refused pair (endpoint + claims) cannot be re-inspected unchanged — change something first. Challenge is for anyone who doubts an issued passport, once a day per agent: the same battery and the same judges, but only a contradiction changes anything. A passport can be taken away with evidence, never parked on a bad day. Either way each validator sends the battery to the endpoint itself, and the round settles only if all of them say the same words.
